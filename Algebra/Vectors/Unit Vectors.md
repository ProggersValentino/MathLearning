##### What is a Unit Vector?
A unit vector is a vector that can go **any direction** but has a magnitude of $1$. these are also known as **normalized vectors**

A unit vector is denoted by the $\Large{\hat{}}$   symbol above the letter: $\hat{u}$

Unit vectors can be represented both in vector notation and matrix notation:

$\begin{aligned} \hat{u} = (1, 1) \\[2em] \hat{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \\[1em] \hat{y} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \end{aligned}$

Generally however, in math's the unit vectors are represented in the notations of:

$\hat{x} = \hat{i} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$

$\hat{y} = \hat{j} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$
$\hat{z} = \hat{k} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$

which its magnitude will also be $1$

Notice that if we put $\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k}$ into one matrix it forms the [identity matrix of R3](Identity%20Matrix) which are the [standard basis vectors](Linear%20Subspaces)

These vectors are essential for normalization calculations.

A unit vector can be calculated by applying the formula:

$\Large{\hat{u} = {(\vec{a}x, \; \vec{a}y) \over ||\vec{a}||}}$

and then to normalize it to $1$ you need get the **magnitude** of the unit vector following:
$\Large{||\hat{u}||=\sqrt{{{\vec{a}x}^2 \; + \; {\vec{a}y}^2 \over ||\vec{a}||}}}$
$\Large{||\hat{u}|| = 1}$
